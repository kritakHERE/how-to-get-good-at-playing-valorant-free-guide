chapter 1

## The big picture

Valorant is two games stacked on top of each other: a gunfight game and a decision game. Most people only train the first one.

Two teams of five, one side attacks and carries the Spike, the other defends. First to 13 rounds wins. Every agent has abilities, but the guns work like a classic tactical shooter: **your first bullet is your best bullet**, and it is only accurate if you are standing still. That one rule explains almost everything in chapters 2 and 3.

#### Layer 1: the duel

Aim, movement, crosshair placement. Wins you single fights. Trainable in 20 minutes a day.

#### Layer 2: the round

Information, map control, trades, utility timing. Wins you rounds even when you aim the same as everyone else.

#### Layer 3: the match

Economy, momentum, adapting, staying calm. Wins you the 13th round.

Every chapter below lives somewhere on this strip.

chapter 2

## Movement

In Valorant, moving and shooting fight each other. Movement is how you choose *when* to be accurate.

Moving makes your bullets spread. Standing still makes them land where you aim, but a still target is easy to hit. So good movement is a rhythm: move, stop, shoot, move again. Strafing means sliding sideways with A and D. One current guide points out that Valorant stops your character almost instantly when you release a key, which is different from CS2 and is why plain stop-and-shoot already works well.

The most common mistake is firing a split second too early.

### The Movement Error trick

Turn on **Show Movement Error** in Settings, Video, Interface. A little indicator around your crosshair shows when you are not truly still. Strafe in the Range, release the key, and only fire once the indicator disappears. This makes the invisible rule visible.

### Movement states at a glance

| State | Accuracy | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing still | best | every real duel |
| Crouching | high, but slow and you cannot retreat | rare close fights (see the Bronze warning in chapter 9) |
| Walking (shift) | good | quiet rotations, enemies cannot hear you |
| Running | bad | repositioning only, never shooting |
| In the air | very bad | positional jumps, never duels |

### The peeks

Tap out, look, tap back. Gather info without a duel.

Show a bit more body to pull a sniper shot, then push while they re-scope.

Go wide past the corner into where their crosshair is not.

#### Counter-strafing, honestly

Counter-strafe means tapping the opposite key to stop even faster (moving right? tap A). It helps a little in Valorant, and it is essential in CS2. One guide suggests learning stop-and-shoot first and treating counter-strafe as a bonus until around Platinum or Diamond. Do not get stuck obsessing over it.

**Do not jiggle** into agents who can flash you on the peek (Breach, Skye), when the enemy already knows exactly where you are, or at spots with two angles looking at you. **Do not shoulder peek** against rifles, because there is no punish window.

chapter 3

## Crosshair placement

Aim is not only about your hand. It is about where your crosshair already is when the enemy appears.

Several guides call this the single most important aiming skill. Keep your crosshair at head height, near the centre of the screen, at the exact spot an enemy would step into. Then you only need a tiny horizontal correction instead of a big flick. Your first shot lands, you win, and you never needed amazing reflexes.

Original sketch. Your crosshair travels along an invisible head-height line as you walk.

#### Pre-aim the spot

Before you turn a corner, aim where an enemy would most likely stand, not at the wall. Think about where people usually hold on that map and what the round situation suggests.

#### Clear corners slowly

Peek angles at a steady pace. Sprinting past an angle gives the enemy the first shot. Scope out corner by corner, with your feet planted when you fire.

#### Merge angles

Advanced idea: find a crosshair spot that covers two possible angles at once. It takes experimenting on each map, and it saves a lot of flicking.

#### Play around their crosshair

Enemies also have crosshairs. A jiggle or jump peek makes them adjust, then you swing into the place they were not looking. This turns placement from defence into offence.

### Crosshair and sensitivity, kept simple

- Your crosshair should be **simple, small, static and consistent**. A static one does not change when you move or shoot, so your eyes learn one picture.
- Valorant is slower than many shooters, so a **lower sensitivity** is more forgiving. Most pros use 400 or 800 DPI. One guide puts typical pro eDPI (DPI times in-game sens) between 200 and 400.
- The first shot is the most accurate. Plan your fights around it, then burst or reset.

#### Trick: the Guardian teaches you

The Guardian punishes body shots, so only head-height crosshair placement works. A few deathmatches with only the Guardian tend to improve placement when you switch back to rifles. Another habit from coaches: play a Range exercise with the Sheriff and Guardian on easy and medium bots.

chapter 4

## Game sense

Game sense is predicting what the enemy will do and being already there. It is built from small, learnable pieces.

Six inputs. Train one at a time.

#### Sound

Footsteps, ability sounds and reloads tell you where people are and what they spent. Walking silently when you should be hearing is a habit worth building.

#### Minimap

Glance at it constantly. It shows where teammates are, which sites are empty and where the last enemy was spotted.

#### Timings

How long does a rotation take from A to B on this map? How long does that smoke last? Knowing numbers turns guesses into plans.

#### Utility cooldowns

If you know their flash, drone or wall was just used, that peek is safer now. Learn what every agent can do, then track what they already spent.

#### Economy reads

If their bank looks thin, expect an eco or a force. Adjust your buy and your positions accordingly.

#### Patterns

Players repeat. If they hit B twice in a row, set a trap or stack the third round. Write one pattern down per match.

#### How to grow it

- **Watch pros and high-rank streamers**, and ask why this smoke happened now. One guide even says high-elo streamers help more, because pro games are fully coordinated and your ranked games are not.
- **Review your own replays.** A coaching site claims fewer than one in ten ranked players below Diamond review consistently, and calls it the highest-leverage habit. That is a marketing claim, but the logic holds.
- Keep a simple notes file of lessons and re-read it weekly to spot patterns in your mistakes.

## Strategy

Strategy is the plan for the round. Tactics are the small moves inside it. Here are the words and the ideas, in plain language.

#### Map control

Owning space on the map. More space means more options and fewer surprises for you.

#### Default

Your safe opening shape. You spread out, take small bits of space, gather information, and decide after seeing what happens.

#### Execute

A planned site hit using several abilities at once. You commit together.

#### Fake

Acting like you are going to one place so the enemy rotates, then going somewhere else.

#### Tempo

The speed of the round. Slowing down, or suddenly speeding up, is a weapon.

#### Crossfire

Two defenders covering the same kill zone from different angles. One dies, the other trades.

### Trading and spacing

A **trade** means you kill the enemy who just killed your teammate. One coaching rule of thumb: if you cannot join the fight within about one second, you are close but not in trade range. Do not stand in the same line as your teammate. Stand where you see the same danger from a second angle.

Same corner, two stances. The right side makes the enemy fight two angles, not one stack.

### Money is strategy

| Buy | What it looks like | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full buy | Rifle, armour, abilities | When most of the team can afford it |
| Half / force | SMGs, pistols, some armour and utility | To disrupt them or after a lost pistol, with team agreement |
| Eco / save | Pistol and few abilities | When saving gives you a full buy next round |
| Full eco | Nothing | When even one more round of spending would hurt |

- Match your team. If most people save and one fully buys, that person usually dies for nothing.
- If you have extra credits, buy a weapon for a teammate who is short.
- Repeated force buys spread your money over several weak rounds. Sometimes one clean eco is better.
- Check their money too. A thin enemy bank tells you what is coming.

### Attack and defence in one list each

#### On attack

- Take map control first, then choose a site.
- Use fakes and rotations to move the defenders.
- Commit with your team, never bait for stats.
- Plan the post-plant before you plant.

#### On defence

- Do not fight alone or give free kills.
- Use crossfires and one off-angle at a time (off-angles are good for one-versus-one but risky if they trade you).
- Anchor smart, rotate only on real information.
- Sometimes push for information if you have none.

Team make-up: pick agents that fit together, ideally one of each role. Pros sometimes skip a role or double up. One analyst notes that double controller or double initiator can be very strong in ranked as well.

## War mindset

An old idea from strategy books: the best fights are the ones you set up so well that they barely feel like fights.

These are my own plain-language translations of classic war thinking into Valorant. They are habits, not slogans.

#### 1. Win the information war

Do not commit blind. Drones, sounds, jiggle peeks and teammates’ calls are your scouts. A round decided by information rarely feels hard.

#### 2. Fight where you are strong

Make every duel a one-versus-one in your favour: behind cover, with a second angle ready, using utility to block other lines. Never take on several enemies at once if you can avoid it.

#### 3. Own the tempo

If they expect speed, slow down. If they expect a slow default, burst. Changing pace breaks their rhythm.

#### 4. Deceive

A fake smoke, footsteps, a sound cue, a rotation that is not real. Make them spend utility and attention on the wrong place.

#### 5. Lose the battle to win the war

A saved rifle is a weapon for the next round. A bad retake costs you the round and the gun. Choose battles by what they cost you in the match.

#### 6. Know yourself

Your tilt, your habits, your default mistakes. A calm player who knows their own weak spots beats a talented player who does not.

#### 7. Keep morale up

It is a race to 13, so momentum matters. TenZ’s advice is to stay positive, even after a run of lost rounds.

#### 8. Adapt

One analyst says the gap between Ascendant and Radiant is often adaptation more than raw mechanics. If the same thing fails twice, change it.

#### Tilt rules that actually help

- **Two-loss rule:** after two ranked losses in a row, close the game. A coaching source calls it more valuable than playing on while angry.
- Do not queue straight after a frustrating loss. Take a short break first.
- Tired, hungry, dehydrated players tilt faster. Check yourself before queuing.
- Do not queue just to reach a number. That is the highest-risk reason to queue.
- Avoid blaming teammates. Ask what you could have done to cover their weak spot.

## Decisions and judgment

Judgment is making good decisions when you do not have all the facts. You will never have all the facts in Valorant.

### The five-step loop

#### 1. Scan

What do I *know* (score, money, who is alive, what was used, what I heard)? What am I only guessing?

#### 2. Options

Name at least two ways to play the next ten seconds. If you only see one, you are not deciding, you are reacting.

#### 3. Price

What does being wrong cost? A jiggle peek costs nearly nothing. A rotation you cannot undo costs a lot.

#### 4. Commit

Pick one and say a trigger: “If I see the Spike, we rotate.” Triggers stop you from dithering.

#### 5. Review

After the round, ask if the *decision* was good, separately from whether it worked.

### Good decision, bad outcome?

Judging yourself by results alone makes you copy lucky mistakes and drop good ideas.

### Retake or save?

One of the most expensive decisions in the game. The right answer depends on numbers, enemy positions, available ultimates and utility, time on the Spike, and your economy. Treat this as a rule of thumb, not a law.

Rule of thumb. If you retake, share the timing out loud and swing together.

#### Solo queue comms that help

- Buy phase: say the plan and the money call.
- First 10 to 15 seconds: set the shape. “Default, hit B at 1:10.”
- Decision window: a clear commit or pivot.
- Callouts: agent plus place (“Raze tag 100 mid, Reyna back B”), not just numbers.
- Do not micromanage teammates. Give info, then trust them.

#### Clutch judgment

- Treat every unchecked spot as a threat, but do not waste time over-clearing.
- Use cover and smokes so you only fight one player.
- Never peek where two people can shoot you at the same time.
- Move from cover to cover. Use height (Jett, Omen) when you can.
- Expect off-angles from smart enemies, since one-versus-one means no trade.

## Radiant and pro wisdom

What players at the top say, boiled down. I paraphrase everything and link the sources at the end.

#### TenZ’s ten, short version

1. Learn two or three agents, not all, and not only one.
2. Talk composition with your team.
3. Try to win your unrated games first, since they shape your start.
4. Do not solo queue if you can avoid it.
5. On attack, communicate and do not bait for stats.
6. On defence, avoid careless deaths.
7. Go for impact kills, the ones that win rounds.
8. Stay positive.
9. Study the pros.
10. Do not queue ranked while tilted.

#### Pro tips on utility

- Psalm described using Sova’s Shock Darts in three ways: displace people off an angle, deal damage on set post-plant retake spots, and punish an attacker execute on defence.
- Subroza suggested following the Sova drone on attack and trading off it. Defenders fear it more when Sova has his ultimate.

#### Demon1’s aim routine

A mix of speed, tracking, accuracy and precision work in an aim trainer, then Valorant itself. The big message is consistency: do it often, do it the same way.

#### What IGLs say

Pros like FNS argue that a good in-game leader needs strong mid-round calling and solid defaults. Calling the same hit every round, without defaults, tends to fall behind.

#### Radiant-level communication

Short, precise, and kind. Name the agent and the place. Warn about dead-teammate info (“careful, Garage”). Do not micromanage.

#### Setup basics from Red Bull’s aim guide

Find a low, comfortable sensitivity. A static crosshair. Sit still when you shoot. Treat crosshair placement as the first job of aim.

Radiant is a ladder badge, not a style. Some Radiants are calm placement players, others flick. The shared trait is habits that survive pressure: head-height crosshair, clean stops before shooting, and tidy decisions.

## Rank by rank

Nine tiers, from Iron to Radiant. Open each one to see how people play there, the usual mistakes, and the one thing to fix.

Gold is both the most common tier and the median (Gold II). Immortal and above is under one in a hundred. Source: valking.gg snapshot, 7 June 2026; Radiant from Esports Tales, Sept 2026. Trackers differ a little.

### Iron

about 6.5%

#### Style

Learning the buttons. Running and shooting, random positions, utility used just because it exists.

#### Fix

Understand how guns work, learn the maps, and try every agent in the Range. Then you will leave Iron without much effort.

### Bronze

about 15.1%

#### Style

Crouch-spraying and run-and-gun, with the crosshair on the floor.

#### Fix

Stop crouch-spraying. It feels accurate, but you commit to the fight and cannot retreat. Unbind crouch in deathmatch and keep the crosshair at head height.

### Silver

about 19.4%

#### Style

Lots of spraying and aimless shooting. Utility is used weakly. Aim differences between players are still big.

#### Fix

Be patient and aim for heads. Pick a couple of agents and learn their setups and lineups. Good utility wins rounds here.

### Gold

about 22.4%, the most common tier

#### Style

Decent to good aim, but players are predictable and teamwork is weak.

#### Fix

Play smart: cheeky flanks, trades, and learn the economy. Mastering a sentinel can lock down a site for your team.

### Platinum

about 18.6%

#### Style

For many, the start of elo hell. Overly aggressive players get read easily.

#### Fix

Play patiently and communicate properly (ask for a flash, call smokes). Start thinking about map control, fakes and rotations. Playing with friends helps.

### Diamond

about 12.0%

#### Style

Good aim, game sense, movement and utility. Competitive lobbies.

#### Fix

Team play. Combine utility, trade teammates, call information. Play to win rounds, not for the scoreboard. If your aim lags, add an aim routine.

### Ascendant

about 5.1%

#### Style

Everyone is grinding for Immortal, so wins feel harder.

#### Fix

Fix your bad habits and become a complete player. Review your own games and settle on a role: smoker, entry or sentinel.

### Immortal

about 0.9%

#### Style

Few weaknesses. Players already know what they lack.

#### Fix

Find your flaws and fix them. Reaching Radiant is mostly a long grind of this loop.

### Radiant

about 0.05%, the top few hundred per region

#### Style

Adaptation, clean mechanics under pressure, tidy decisions and strong communication.

#### Fix

Keep the habits from every earlier tier. At this level nobody has an easy rank to hide behind.

Rank rules for teaming up (from PC Gamer): Iron and Bronze can queue with players up to Silver. Silver up to Gold. Gold up to Platinum. From Platinum up, teammates must be exactly one tier apart.

## The agents

Thirty playable agents as of Hush, the ninth Duelist, who arrived in patch 13.09 on 27 October 2026. Every agent belongs to one of four roles. Inside each role, I group them by how they actually play.

Duelist is the biggest role. Hush made it nine.

#### Duelist

The entry team. They make space and start fights. Think: first to move, first to be seen.

**Subclasses:** **Space-takers** who dash or fly into site (Jett, Raze, Waylay). **Flash entry** who blind then go (Phoenix, Yoru, Neon). **Self-sufficient fraggers** who win fights alone and heal from kills (Reyna, Iso). **Precision / displacement** (Hush), who moves enemies and herself.

#### Initiator

The information and set-up team. They find enemies, flush them out and create openings for entries.

**Subclasses:** **Recon** (Sova, Fade, Skye in part). **Flash and stun support** (Breach, Skye, KAY/O, Gekko). **Anti-utility** (KAY/O). **Explosive flushers** who punish hiding (Tejo, Breach).

#### Controller

The smoke and map control team. They cut sightlines so the team can take or hold space.

**Subclasses:** **Global smokers** who place smokes from the map (Brimstone, Astra, Clove, Miks). **On-body smokers** who smoke from where they stand (Omen, Harbor). **Wall / zone controllers** (Viper, Harbor). **Support controller** who also heals and buffs (Miks).

#### Sentinel

The lockdown team. They hold sites, watch flanks and punish people who push the wrong way.

**Subclasses:** **Setup and trap** (Killjoy, Cypher, Deadlock, Vyse). **Healer and wall** (Sage). **Mobile or aggressive** who fight more than they sit (Chamber, Veto).

Roles are Riot’s official labels. Subclasses are the community’s shorthand, and they overlap. Riot’s own designers use “space-taking duelist” for Jett, Raze and Waylay. Clove is listed as a Controller but plays like a Duelist or Initiator, Harbor is often mistaken for an Initiator, and Veto sits in Sentinel but plays aggressively.

#### New in 13.09: two buff words

**Haste** means a movement speed boost, as opposed to a Combat Stim, which boosts fire rate, equip, reload and recovery. **Snapshot** is a still-image silhouette reveal debuff, like Cypher’s Spycam and Neural Theft. You will see these words in the new ability tooltips.

Difficulty labels come from one site’s roster table (esports.gg), so read them as a rough guide, and other sites rate some agents differently. Hush is not rated. Her kit details come from launch coverage, so check the in-game text for exact numbers.

## Practice lab

Repeatable actions beat inspiration. These are small, specific, and each has a finish line so you know when you did it.

### The 20-minute warm-up

| Minutes | Where | What | Pass mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | The Range | Movement Error on. Strafe at bots, stop, shoot one bullet, repeat. | 20 shots in a row with the indicator gone |
| 5 to 10 | Range corners | Jiggle peek a corner, see the bot, stop, shoot, return. | exposure around 0.3 s; never fire while moving |
| 10 to 20 | Deathmatch | Stop-shoot in every duel and keep the crosshair at head height. | you can say where your crosshair was after each death |

Pass marks are my suggestions. The structure follows a published beginner movement plan.

### Ten reps you can repeat

#### 1. Stop-shoot rep

Strafe, stop until Movement Error is gone, fire one bullet. 20 in a row.

#### 2. Corner walk

In a custom game, walk one map and note what sits at head height at every corner. One map per day.

#### 3. Jiggle ladder

Ten short jiggles at each of five angles. Vary the rhythm so you are not predictable.

#### 4. Guardian deathmatch

One match with only the Guardian. Count headshots, not kills.

#### 5. No-crouch deathmatch

Unbind crouch for a match. Notice how you survive without it.

#### 6. Pre-aim path

Choose a route on a map. Pre-aim each common spot as you move. Repeat five times.

#### 7. Call it first

Say aloud where you think the enemy is before you peek. Check yourself afterwards.

#### 8. Trade drill

With a friend: one enters, one trades. Practise the one-second window until it feels easy.

#### 9. Three lineups

Pick one agent and one map. Learn three setups or lineups and repeat each five times.

#### 10. Death review

After each match, watch one death and answer: what did I know, what did I assume, what would I change?

### A simple week

| Day | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Mon | Warm-up, then a few ranked games. Review one death. |
| Tue | Warm-up, Guardian deathmatch, corner walk on one map. |
| Wed | Warm-up, ranked. Practise “call it first”. |
| Thu | Lineups for one agent, trade drill if you have a friend. |
| Fri | Warm-up, ranked with the two-loss rule on. |
| Sat | Watch a pro or Radiant stream. Write down three decisions you would not have made. |
| Sun | Rest, or review replays and update your notes file. |

#### Before every round

- What is the money call?
- What is our shape for the first ten seconds?
- What will I do if I get information early?

#### During the round

- Head-height crosshair on every step.
- Stop before shooting.
- Check the minimap each time you pause.
- Say one useful callout.

#### After the round

- What did I know that I ignored?
- Was it a good call or just a good result?
- One thing for next round only.

#### Session rules

- Warm up before ranked.
- Two losses in a row, close the game.
- Eat, drink, then queue.
- Set a game limit before you start.
